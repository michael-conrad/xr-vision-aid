## Vergence demand by viewing distance

Approximate values for a 6 cm pupillary distance; relative comparison holds across any PD.

| Viewing situation | Distance | Vergence demand |
|---|---|---|
| Dome/stand magnifier image | ~25 cm | ~12 prism diopters (PD) |
| Typical adult reading | 29–40 cm | ~7.5–10 PD |
| Monitor zone (proven comfortable) | 60–90 cm (2–3 ft) | ~3.5–5 PD |
| MaxDetail max reach | 71 cm (28 in) | ~4–5 PD |

## Conclusions

1. Non-CI readers voluntarily read at 29–40 cm; the ~25 cm stand-magnifier image distance sits in the zone normal readers avoid drifting toward. It is comfortable only for users without vergence pathology.
2. The intervention should move effective vergence demand into the **4–5 PD zone** — the same regime as the monitor viewing already tolerated for hours — or train the vergence system (vision therapy) to tolerate near.
3. The Eschenbach MaxDetail's 28" maximum working distance lands almost exactly in the monitor-comfort zone: ~4–5 PD at 2.1x magnification. This is why it is the correct optical tool: it is not "more magnification," it is moving the demand down the gradient.

## Standard reading distance facts

- Clinical convention: 40 cm (16") is the canonical near testing/reading distance (Ophthalmic & Physiological Optics, "Why do we test at 40 cm?"; Vision Science Academy).
- Measured adult behavior: adults typically start a 30-minute reading task at ~32 cm, drifting to ~29 cm as they fatigue — closer than 40 cm, not farther.
- Consumer guidance: "about forty centimetres" comfortable adult range (bluefinvision knowledge base).
